Abstract

Drug Zeta increased hospitalization compared with placebo in patients with heart failure (hazard ratio 1.12, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.24; p=0.03).

Introduction

A previous trial by Hartmann et al. [3] reported that Drug Zeta reduced hospitalization compared with placebo (hazard ratio 0.55, 95% CI 0.40 to 0.76). Whether this holds in heart failure is unknown.

Methods

In this double-blind trial, 3,870 patients with heart failure were randomized to Drug Zeta or placebo and followed for a median of 2.1 years.

Results

Drug Zeta increased hospitalization compared with placebo in patients with heart failure (hazard ratio 1.12, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.24; p=0.03). Serious adverse events were balanced between groups.

Discussion

The excess in hospitalizations was driven by worsening heart failure events.
