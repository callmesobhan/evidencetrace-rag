# Independent (blind) annotation

The M1 gold standard was produced by one LLM that was also the extractor's developer. This directory holds the
first check that does not have that property: **five separate LLM agents, each with its own context, annotated 36
papers without ever seeing the system's output or the developer's gold.** They are LLM agents, not humans.
Nothing here has been verified by a person, and an LLM annotator can share blind spots with the LLM that wrote
the system. What this evidence adds is *independence of context*, not human ground truth.

## Protocol

1. 61 papers were given opaque ids (`P01`..`P61`; the id → PMCID map is `pack/blind_map.json`, which the annotators
   did not have). 55 are **fresh** papers (CC BY, found by a new PubMed query, never seen during development,
   `../fresh_manifest.jsonl`); 6 are **agreement** papers already in the developer's TEST split, hidden among the
   fresh ones. 36 of the 61 were assigned (`pack/chosen.json`): 30 fresh + the 6 agreement papers.
2. The system's predictions on all 61 papers were generated by the code frozen in `../frozen_snapshot_fresh/` and
   **hashed before any annotation existed** (`../FROZEN.json` → `fresh_holdout`).
3. The annotators got the papers' text and `../../../docs/annotation-guidelines.md` (v1) only, 7-8 papers each
   (`pack/assignment_k.txt`), and wrote `pack/out/annotations_k.jsonl` (in-scope estimates) and
   `pack/out/out_of_scope_k.jsonl` (CI-bearing quantities they excluded, with a category).
4. `scripts/score_blind.py` scores (a) the independent annotation against the developer's gold on the 6 agreement
   papers and (b) the frozen system against the independent annotation on the 30 fresh papers, with a bootstrap
   over papers.

Files: `predictions_prefix.jsonl` (held-out system output, hash-pinned), `predictions_postreview.jsonl` (system
output after fixes made *because of* this exercise), `scores_<variant>/independent_scores.json` and
`disagreements.jsonl` (every system-only / annotator-only estimate, with its sentence).
`make independent` regenerates and diffs all of them.

`pack/out/annotations_{1..5}.jsonl` contain 20 records for papers the writing annotator was **not** assigned
(P04: 9, P08: 6, P13: 5): they are leftovers of a first annotator wave that died on a session limit before finishing, and
`score_blind.py` drops any record whose paper is not in that annotator's assignment (`stale_records_dropped_not_assigned`
in the score files). The 36th paper (PMC7902189) has no in-scope estimate according to its annotator.

## Results

Value + CI match (metric, point estimate and CI bounds equal), 95% bootstrap CI over papers in brackets.

**A. Held-out: the system as frozen before the annotation, on 30 fresh papers (196 independent gold estimates).**

| | precision | recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 30 papers | 0.859 [0.721, 0.962] | 0.653 [0.507, 0.805] | **0.742** [0.609, 0.865] |
| 15 cardiometabolic (the domain M1 was built on) | 0.933 | 0.874 | **0.902** [0.827, 0.962] |
| 15 other domains | 0.689 | 0.365 | **0.477** [0.284, 0.692] |

Attribution on matched findings: 1 of 139 "own" labels was another study's result (0.7%), kappa 0.89 against the
independent own/cited judgement.

**B. Agreement between the two annotators on the 6 overlap papers** (independent vs developer gold, 90 vs 52
estimates): F1 0.648 strict, 0.718 if "incidence rate ratio" and "rate ratio" are treated as one metric; attribution
kappa 1.0. One paper dominates: **PMC12810801 reports 35 odds ratios as `COR/p = 6.30/0.01`** (no CI), which the
independent annotator recorded and the system and the developer's gold both missed. The developer's earlier test
recall of 0.95 was therefore **optimistic**: it could not see notations the developer never encountered.

**C. After the fixes (tuned on these same 30 papers, so NOT held-out):** F1 0.865 [0.799, 0.930]
(precision 0.963, recall 0.786); cardiometabolic 0.920, other domains 0.783. (An earlier version of this table said
0.871: three predictions shared a `finding_id`, which the scorer used as a key. The scorer now keys by row; the
held-out figures in A were unaffected.)

## What the exercise found (and fixed)

Real extractor gaps, all in the held-out predictions: credible intervals (`CrI`, 15 estimates), least-squares mean
differences (`LSMD`, 6), Cohen's d with a CI (2), `mean difference at week 52, -2.91; 95% CI` (3), `absolute difference
in risk` (1), a DOI parsed as an RR (false positive), within-arm changes parsed as MD (3 false positives), and **a sign
loss in the frozen extractor**: `difference of – 3.35 g/m2` was returned as +3.35 (an en dash after a filler phrase was
eaten). The sign-loss bug was present in the code behind the held-out test numbers in `../results/`; it is fixed in
current code and covered by a regression test, and `docs/known-issues.md` states how many test findings it could
have affected.

## What it did not settle

- Scope disagreements are not extractor errors: the annotators recorded some unnamed quantities ("effect size
  0.24", unnamed differences) that the guidelines exclude, and flagged 22 records `uncertain`. Part of the remaining
  recall gap is that.
- Notations like `COR/p = 6.30/0.01` are still not extracted (a deliberate non-fix: tuning to one paper's idiosyncratic
  notation would overfit).
- Thirty papers, five annotators, one rater per paper: the intervals above are wide, and there is no inter-rater
  statistic across annotators except on the 6 overlap papers.
